Static layer

Blocks are solid slabs whose lower edge breaks into drifting, shrinking fragments. Most gather in one area; Left-edge blocks adds a set cut off by the left side of the frame. Image colour bleeds down from the picture into the static; raise it to carry the colour deeper.

Each grain of static is drawn in black and white or in one of the three colours. The sliders set how much of each goes into the mix: 0 leaves that colour out, and the bar above shows the balance. Set black and white to 0 for all-colour static.

Background distortion

Pull tears the picture harder close to the static front, so the two effects meet instead of butting together. It applies to the subject too.

Subject cut-out layer

The cut-out is layered over the full picture and torn on its own bands, so it can break up while the background stays calm, or the other way round. The vertical and horizontal sliders move and size the area of heaviest tearing on the subject (a dashed box shows the area while you drag). Vertical position moves it up or down the subject and carries the fine streaky detail with it; Vertical height widens it until it covers the whole subject. Distortion goes past 100% for extreme breakup.

The same mix as the background colour, but only for static that lands on the hand and shears: the static covering them, the static edge fraying into them and the fine streaks inside the distortion area. Static on subject sprinkles dashes of static across the whole cut-out, wherever the static front is; Black and white sets how much of that static is black and white rather than colour. Tick Same as background to use one mix for everything.

The CRT stage runs after everything else, so it treats static, picture and subject as one signal. Bright areas swell into the scanline gaps and glow; the vignette always reaches its full darkness right at the frame edge, and falloff sets how late it starts; colour trails to the right and the red and blue guns sit slightly off; the wave pushes whole lines sideways, and Wave reach / build confine it to the bottom of the frame where the static sits.

Output

Video

Video is rendered frame by frame at full size, so it plays smoothly however long each frame takes. Static rises starts on the clean picture and builds every effect (colour, blocks, background and subject distortion, wave and tears) up to your settings while the static rises to cover the whole frame; Clears runs it the other way. Noise changes every 2 frames feels like real tape; 1 is busier.
